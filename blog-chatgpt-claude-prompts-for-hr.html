10 Practical ChatGPT & Claude Prompts for HR Teams

AI for HRBy GRC Training Online · November 26, 2026

AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude can take hours off routine HR writing, but only if you ask them the right way. Below are ten prompts you can copy and adapt today, a simple structure for writing your own, and the ground rules that keep AI use safe in a function that handles sensitive information.

A simple structure for any HR prompt

Most weak AI output comes from vague requests. A good HR prompt has five parts:

  1. Role: who the AI should act as ("You are an experienced HR generalist at a 200-person U.S. company").
  2. Context: the facts it needs: industry, location, audience, the policy or situation.
  3. Task: exactly what you want produced.
  4. Format: bullets, a table, an email, a one-page summary.
  5. Constraints: tone, length, reading level, and what to avoid.

Every prompt below follows this pattern. Replace the text in [brackets] with your own details.

Ground rules before you start

  • Keep personal data out. Don't paste employee names, medical information, pay details or anything that identifies a person. Describe the situation in general terms instead.
  • Check your company's AI policy and the data settings of the tool you use.
  • Treat output as a first draft. AI can state laws and deadlines confidently and still be wrong or out of date. Verify anything legal against the source or with counsel.
  • Review for bias, especially in anything touching hiring, performance or discipline.

The 10 prompts

1. Write a job description

You are an HR business partner. Write a job description for a [job title] at a [size, industry] company in [state]. Include a 3-sentence summary, 6–8 responsibilities, required and preferred qualifications, and the work arrangement [onsite/hybrid/remote]. Use inclusive, plain language and avoid unnecessary degree requirements.

Then ask: "Flag any wording that could discourage qualified applicants."

2. Build structured interview questions

Create 8 structured, job-related interview questions for a [job title], focused on [skills]. For each, add what a strong answer would include. Avoid any question about age, family status, health, religion or other protected characteristics.

3. Summarize a policy in plain English

Rewrite the policy below as a one-page summary for employees at an 8th-grade reading level. Keep every requirement and deadline, use short bullets, and end with "Who to contact with questions." [paste policy text]

4. Create an onboarding checklist

Create a first-90-days onboarding checklist for a new [job title], split into before day 1, week 1, month 1 and months 2–3. Assign each item to HR, the manager or the new hire, in a table.

5. Prepare for a difficult conversation

I'm a manager preparing to discuss [general issue, e.g. repeated late arrivals] with an employee. Give me an opening statement, 3 open questions, likely responses and how to handle each, and how to close with clear next steps. Keep it respectful and factual.

6. Tighten performance documentation

Review this draft note for objectivity. Point out opinions, labels or assumptions, and rewrite it to describe only observable behavior, dates, impact and the expectation going forward. [paste anonymized draft]

Good documentation is factual, specific and timely. AI is a useful second pair of eyes, but the facts have to come from you.

7. Find themes in survey comments

Here are anonymized employee survey comments. Group them into 5–7 themes, count how many comments fall under each, quote 2 representative comments per theme, and suggest one action per theme. [paste comments]

8. Outline a training session

Outline a 60-minute training session for [audience] on [topic]. Include learning objectives, a timed agenda, one short exercise and 5 quiz questions to check understanding.

9. Draft an employee announcement

Write a short, warm email announcing [change, e.g. a new PTO request process] to all employees. Explain what is changing, when, why, and what they need to do. Under 200 words, with a clear subject line.

10. Run a first-pass policy gap check

Compare our handbook section below against this checklist of topics: [list]. Show which topics are covered, which are missing and which are unclear, in a table. Do not give legal conclusions. [paste section]

Use the result as a to-do list for your own review or for counsel, never as a compliance sign-off.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Asking one-line questions and accepting the first answer. Follow up: "make it shorter", "add a table", "what did you assume?"
  • Trusting legal details without checking them. Leave laws, thresholds and deadlines to the official source.
  • Using AI to make decisions about people. Use it to draft, organize and summarize; keep the judgment human.

Frequently asked questions

Can HR use ChatGPT or Claude for confidential employee matters?

Avoid pasting names, medical details, compensation or other personal data into a public AI tool. Remove identifying details first, and check your organization's AI use policy and your tool's data settings.

Are AI-written policies legally reliable?

No. AI is useful for a first draft or a plain-English summary, but an HR professional and, where needed, employment counsel should review anything with legal consequences before it is used.

What is the best prompt structure for HR tasks?

Give the AI a role, the context, a clear task, the output format you want and any constraints, such as length, tone or what to leave out.
